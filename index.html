Research

My doctoral thesis is on constrained reinforcement learning: I design efficient and frugal algorithms for sequential decision-making under uncertainty, with provable guarantees. A central question is how constraints, whether on safety, resources or preferences, and whether known or to be learned, change the statistical hardness of bandit and reinforcement learning problems. I study it through sample-complexity lower bounds and algorithms that match them.

My interests also span fair machine learning, where fairness requirements act as constraints on the learner. Lately I have been working on performative machine learning and reinforcement learning, in which a deployed model or policy shifts the environment it learns from, and on dynamic fairness and auditing, where bias is measured and mitigated over time as models, data and populations evolve.

I am advised by Debabrota Basu and Odalric-Ambrym Maillard.
